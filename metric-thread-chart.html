How the numbers are calculated

ISO 724 gives the basic profile of the metric thread from the nominal diameter d and the pitch P. Every value in the table is calculated from these two numbers, so you can check any row yourself:

pitch diameter d2 = d − 0.649519·P minor diameter bolt d3 = d − 1.226869·P minor diameter nut D1 = d − 1.082532·P stress area As = π/4 · ((d2 + d3)/2)² tap drill (rule) ≈ d − P

Example, M10 × 1.5: d2 = 10 − 0.974 = 9.026 mm, d3 = 8.160 mm, D1 = 8.376 mm, As = 58.0 mm². The tap drill is 8.5 mm.

Coarse or fine pitch?

Use the coarse pitch by default: it is faster to assemble, less sensitive to damage and every nut and tap is in stock. Fine pitches give a larger stress area, finer adjustment and better self-locking — use them for thin walls, adjusting screws and high-strength bolts where the extra strength counts.

Wrench and key sizes

Hex sizes are the ISO 4032 / ISO 4014 values. Older DIN 934 / DIN 933 hardware uses 17 mm for M10, 19 mm for M12, 22 mm for M14 and 32 mm for M22 — check which standard your supplier delivers.

Frequently asked questions

What is the pitch of an M8 bolt?

The coarse (standard) pitch of M8 is 1.25 mm. Fine pitches are 1 mm and 0.75 mm, written as M8×1 or M8×0.75.

What size drill for an M10 tap?

8.5 mm for the standard M10×1.5 thread (d − P). For M10×1.25 use 8.75 mm, for M10×1 use 9 mm.

What does M12×1.5 mean?

A metric thread with 12 mm nominal diameter and a fine pitch of 1.5 mm. Without the “×P” (just M12) the coarse pitch, 1.75 mm, is meant.

What is the stress area used for?

The tensile stress area As is the cross-section used to calculate bolt strength: proof load = proof stress × As. For M12, As = 84.3 mm², so an 8.8 bolt (proof stress 580 MPa) carries about 48.9 kN before permanent stretch.
