Why the same gauge has different thickness

A gauge number is not a size — it is a convention, and each material has its own:

  • Steel uses the Manufacturers’ Standard Gauge, based on weight: 41.82 lb/ft² for each inch of thickness. 10 ga steel weighs 5.625 lb/ft², so it is 5.625 / 41.82 = 0.1345 in.
  • Galvanized steel adds the zinc coating: 2.5 oz/ft² more weight, so the same gauge is a little thicker (10 ga = 0.1382 in).
  • Stainless steel follows the older US Standard Gauge in fractions of an inch (16 ga = 1/16 in = 0.0625 in).
  • Aluminum uses the American Wire Gauge (Brown & Sharpe): d = 0.005 × 92(36 − n)/39 in, so 10 ga = 0.1019 in.

Always quote and order sheet by thickness, not gauge — and check the actual thickness with the supplier: mills deliver within a tolerance around the nominal value.

Frequently asked questions

How thick is 16 gauge steel?

0.0598 in (1.52 mm) for carbon steel. 16 ga stainless is 0.0625 in (1.59 mm), 16 ga aluminum is 0.0508 in (1.29 mm).

How thick is 10 gauge steel in mm?

3.42 mm (0.1345 in). Galvanized 10 ga is 3.51 mm.

Is a higher gauge number thicker?

No — the higher the gauge number, the thinner the sheet. 22 ga is thinner than 16 ga.

What metric thickness is close to 14 gauge?

14 ga steel is 0.0747 in = 1.90 mm; the nearest common metric sheet is 2 mm.
