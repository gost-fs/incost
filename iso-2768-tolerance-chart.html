How to read “ISO 2768-mK” on a drawing

The first letter is the class for linear and angular dimensions (ISO 2768-1), the second — for geometrical tolerances (ISO 2768-2). mK means: medium class for sizes, class K for flatness, straightness, perpendicularity, symmetry and run-out. A 100 mm dimension without its own tolerance may then be 99.7–100.3 mm.

Which class for which process

  • f — precision machining, ground parts.
  • m — normal machining and laser cutting; the most common choice.
  • c — welded frames, plasma cutting, rough machining.
  • v — rough fabrication, flame cutting, castings before machining.

Tighter general tolerances cost money on every dimension of the part — specify a fine tolerance only on the features that need it, and keep the general class as coarse as the function allows.

Frequently asked questions

What is the ISO 2768-m tolerance for 100 mm?

±0.3 mm (range 30–120 mm, medium class).

What does ISO 2768-mK mean?

Medium class (m) for linear and angular dimensions per ISO 2768-1 and class K for geometrical tolerances per ISO 2768-2.

What is the general tolerance for a 10 mm dimension in class f?

±0.1 mm (range 6–30 mm, fine class).

Is ISO 2768 still valid?

ISO 2768-2 was withdrawn in favour of ISO 22081, but ISO 2768 general tolerances are still widely used on drawings and in shop agreements.
