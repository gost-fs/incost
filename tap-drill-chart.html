How to choose a tap drill

For a metric cutting tap the rule is simple: drill = nominal diameter − pitch. M8×1.25 → 6.8 mm, M6×1 → 5 mm. A few sizes are rounded to the nearest standard drill (M12 → 10.2 mm, M2.5 → 2.05 mm).

For inch threads the drill is picked for about 75% of the full thread height — stronger than needed for most joints, but still easy on the tap. The percentage of thread for any drill is:

thread % = (major diameter − drill diameter) × TPI / 0.01299

Hard or tough materials (stainless, titanium) and deep holes: choose a drill that gives 60–65% of thread. Soft materials and short engagements: 75–80%.

Forming taps

Roll-forming taps do not cut chips and need a larger hole, roughly d − P/2. Use the tap maker’s table for the exact size.

Frequently asked questions

What size drill for an M6 tap?

5 mm for M6×1 (coarse). For M6×0.75 use 5.25 mm.

What drill size for a 1/4-20 tap?

#7 drill (0.201 in, 5.11 mm) — about 75% thread. A 13/64 in drill (0.203 in) is a close substitute.

What drill for M8 tap?

6.8 mm for M8×1.25. For M8×1 use 7 mm.

Why is the tap drill smaller than the bolt?

The tap cuts the thread profile into the wall of the hole, so the hole must be about the size of the minor diameter of the thread: nominal diameter minus one pitch.
